# Figure legends

## Figure 1. Sampling design and recorded site characteristics

(a) Deposited coordinates of the nine composite soil units, with degradation category and locality from the analytical metadata. (b) Numbers of units by degradation category and locality. (c) Recorded vegetation, preserving the distinction between grassland and pasture. Teal, ochre and magenta indicate low, moderate and high degradation, respectively; circles indicate Jaén (J) and triangles San Ignacio (SI). Both markers characterize the same nine units, with three units per degradation category. Coordinates were supplied in BioSample records; administrative boundaries and field independence were not established from these coordinates. Low-degradation units occur only in Jaén, and vegetation differs among categories.

## Figure 2. Alpha diversity across soil degradation categories

Observed amplicon sequence variant (ASV) richness (a, b) and Shannon diversity, using natural logarithms (c, d), for 16S (a, c) and ITS (b, d). Points represent nine soil units (three per category); horizontal lines are category medians. Teal, ochre and magenta denote low, moderate and high degradation; circles denote Jaén (J) and triangles San Ignacio (SI). The 16S estimates use one rarefaction draw at 174,661 sequences per unit; ITS estimates average 100 draws at 450,343 sequences. ITS ranges are technical 2.5th–97.5th percentiles, which may be narrower than the symbols, rather than biological confidence intervals. Category tests condition on locality and enumerate 4,320 within-locality arrangements. Annotations give raw P, Holm-adjusted P and partial R² after locality adjustment. Holm families contain three 16S and four ITS endpoints. No alpha endpoint has adjusted P < 0.05. Marker-specific richness estimates are not directly comparable organism counts.

## Figure 3. Community composition and within-category dispersion

Principal coordinates analysis (PCoA) of Bray–Curtis dissimilarities from (a) rarefied 16S prokaryotic counts and (b) complete ITS fungal-library proportions. Axes preserve equal geometric scales; percentages refer to positive inertia. Category tests condition on locality, with 4,320 within-locality arrangements and effect/residual degrees of freedom of 2/5. Annotations distinguish raw P from Holm-adjusted P, covering three 16S and four ITS category endpoints. (c, d) Distances to category spatial medians, corrected for small-sample bias; horizontal bars indicate median distances. PERMDISP uses degrees of freedom of 2/6 and within-locality permutations, without conditioning the dispersion model on locality. Its 16S P value is unadjusted; the ITS P value belongs to a separate two-diagnostic Holm family. All panels show the same nine soil units. Teal, ochre and magenta denote low, moderate and high degradation; circles denote Jaén (J) and triangles San Ignacio (SI). PCoA is a descriptive projection, not the fitted conditional model.

## Figure 4. Phylum-level composition of retained 16S and ITS libraries

Bars show sequence proportions in each of nine soil units before rarefaction, using the complete retained prokaryotic library for 16S (a) and explicitly assigned fungal library for ITS (b). Ten leading resolved phylum labels per marker were selected by mean sample proportion; remaining labels form Other, and unresolved assignments remain explicit. Classifications use SILVA 138.1 at confidence 0.70 and UNITE 10 at confidence 0.90. Segment labels show one-decimal percentages when abundance is at least 5%; smaller segments remain visible. Supplementary Tables S19 and S20 provide all proportions and denominators. Rounding may prevent displayed labels from summing exactly to 100%. Sample-label colors denote low (teal), moderate (ochre) and high (magenta) degradation; circles denote Jaén (J) and triangles San Ignacio (SI). These are descriptive sequence proportions, not absolute organism abundances; no category significance test is attached to this display.

## Supplementary Figure S02. Soil properties across degradation categories

## Supplementary Figure S07. Covariation among recorded soil properties

## Supplementary Figure S08. ASV intersections among degradation categories

Intersections of repeatedly detected amplicon sequence variants (ASVs), calculated separately for 16S and ITS. An ASV enters a category-specific set when at least one retained sequence occurs in at least two of its three soil units. Bars show mutually exclusive intersections, and connected dots identify participating categories. Counts use complete retained, unrarefied libraries; ASVs failing the within-category detection rule are reported separately. These depth-sensitive inventories are descriptive and do not test category differences in richness. The two markers represent distinct ASV sets and are not intersected with each other. Shared detections do not establish a functional core or species identity. Sample-level rarefied richness is reported in Figure 2.

## Supplementary Figure S11. Fungal community sensitivity to balanced error learning

## Supplementary Figure S12. Update history of balanced ITS error learning

Changes in supported substitution-error probabilities during DADA2 1.30.0 error learning from 1,196,903 read pairs distributed across nine libraries. (a) Maximum absolute change between successive updates. (b) Absolute change weighted by terminal transition support. Colors, symbols and line styles distinguish forward R1 and reverse R2 reads, not degradation categories or localities. Outlined symbols mark the final three updates. Positive changes share logarithmic axis limits; the exact reverse zero at update nine appears in a separate zero strip without substitution by a positive floor. All ten forward and nine reverse updates are shown. Forward learning reached the ten-update limit without an exact fixed point; reverse learning reached one at update nine. These are diagnostics for the balanced sensitivity model, not the convergence history of the primary model. Small numerical changes alone do not demonstrate error calibration.

## Supplementary Figure S14. NMDS representations of community dissimilarity

Two-dimensional nonmetric multidimensional scaling (NMDS) of the primary Bray–Curtis matrices for 16S and ITS. Top panels show complete configurations with equal geometric scales; bottom panels magnify the nearly coincident clusters without jitter. All nine units remain in the full panels. Teal, ochre and magenta denote low, moderate and high degradation; circles denote Jaén (J) and triangles San Ignacio (SI). Each fit completed 100 random starts, yielding stress-1 values of 0.0000808124 for 16S and 0.0000711906 for ITS. Both fits produced near-zero-stress warnings and nearly one-dimensional configurations. Different magnification scales cannot be interpreted as relative ecological effect sizes. NMDS axes have no explained-variance percentages and add no hypothesis test. The PCoA analyses in Figure 3 remain the primary ordinations; Supplementary Figure S15 shows the corresponding Shepard diagnostics.

## Supplementary Figure S15. Shepard diagnostics for the NMDS fits

Observed Bray–Curtis dissimilarities plotted against fitted ordination distances for all 36 distinct pairs of the nine soil units in each marker. Points are sample pairs, not independent biological replicates. Lines show the native monotonic fit from monoMDS without smoothing or omitted pairs. Ordination distances retain the solver scale; Supplementary Figure S14 uses uniformly rescaled site coordinates, which leave stress unchanged. The step-like mapping compresses some dissimilar pairs into nearly coincident ordination points. Consequently, the near-zero stress values do not by themselves establish an informative two-dimensional representation. Fit diagnostics are in Supplementary Table S09, and pairwise values are in Supplementary Table S10. This diagnostic introduces no additional significance test.
